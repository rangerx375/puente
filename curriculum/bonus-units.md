# Instructions: Career & Interest-Based English Units

## Context
- Students are Spanish speakers in **El Valle de Antón, Panama (Coclé province)**. All content should use local places, situations, and realities.
- Goal: practical, real-world English students can use at work and in daily life with English-speaking clients, tourists, and residents.
- **Local customs to include where relevant:**
  - **Jubilado discount** (retiree discount): customers often ask for it at restaurants, hotels, and many businesses. Students need to explain it, ask for ID, and apply it. The **grocery store does NOT give the jubilado discount**, so students must also be able to explain that politely.
  - **Punto de Oro:** the customer loyalty card at **El Rey** supermarket. Customers often ask about it, so include it in the Grocery Store and Cashier units.
- Units must be **beefy**: large vocabulary sets and many sentence structures so students learn *how to say* what they need, not just isolated words.

## Standard Structure for Every Unit
1. **Vocabulary bank** – 100–150+ terms, grouped by category (tools, materials, actions, problems, people, places). Include Spanish equivalent, part of speech, and one example sentence each.
2. **Key phrases & sentence frames** – 40+ reusable patterns with blanks (e.g., "I need to ___ the ___ because ___.").
3. **Grammar focus** – 2–3 structures most useful for that field (e.g., imperatives, modals, past tense for reporting, questions), taught through the unit's vocabulary.
4. **Dialogues** – 5–8 realistic conversations set in El Valle, from simple to more complex.
5. **Role-play scenarios** – 5+ situations for students to act out, with both roles described.
6. **Listening/reading practice** – short texts, messages, emails, or forms typical of the field.
7. **Exercises** – matching, fill-in-the-blank, sentence building, translation (Spanish → English), and question-answer practice.
8. **Common mistakes** – errors Spanish speakers typically make in this area (false friends, word order, pronunciation).
9. **Review quiz** – vocabulary + phrases + a short speaking task.

Levels: provide a **basic** and **intermediate** version of dialogues and exercises where possible.

## Unit 1: Construction & Home Repair
- **Tools:** hammer, drill, screwdriver, level, tape measure, saw, trowel, caulk gun, wrench, pliers, wheelbarrow, ladder, shovel, grinder.
- **Materials:** cement, concrete block, rebar, sand, gravel, tile, grout, caulk, silicone, PVC pipe, fittings, zinc/metal roofing, plywood, paint, primer, sealant, drywall, wire, breaker.
- **Problems:** leaky shower, clogged drain, roof leak, mold/mildew (rainy season humidity), cracked wall, water damage, broken tile, low water pressure, water tank issues, power outage, termites, septic tank problems.
- **Language tasks:** explaining what's wrong and what needs to be done ("The shower is leaking, so I need to remove the old caulk and reseal it"); giving **quotes/estimates** (materials + labor, time needed); scheduling; asking clarifying questions; explaining step-by-step how a job is done; reporting when a job is finished; buying at the hardware store.
- **Grammar:** "need to / have to," imperatives, sequencing (first, then, after that), future (will / going to), numbers, measurements, and prices.

## Unit 2: Gardening & Landscaping
- **Tools:** machete, weed whacker/trimmer, lawn mower, rake, hoe, pruning shears, hose, sprinkler, wheelbarrow, gloves, blower.
- **Plants & terms:** lawn, hedge, shrub, orchid, palm, fruit tree, flowers, seeds, seedlings, soil, compost, fertilizer, mulch, pots, weeds, pests, roots, leaves, drainage.
- **Language tasks:** understanding client requests ("Please trim the hedge but leave the orchids"); asking about plant care, watering, sunlight; explaining plant problems (fungus, bugs, too much rain); recommending plants for El Valle's climate; giving prices and schedules; describing seasons (rainy vs. dry).
- **Grammar:** imperatives, "should / shouldn't," frequency adverbs (every week, twice a month), comparatives.

## Unit 3: Tourism in El Valle
- **Setting:** El Valle sits inside an ancient volcanic crater, surrounded by mountains. It has a cool climate, rainforest, and over 300 bird species.
- **Hikes (include name, difficulty, time, what you'll see, what to bring):**
  - **La India Dormida** (moderate): legend of the "Sleeping Indian" girl, crater-rim views, waterfall, pre-Columbian rock carvings.
  - **Cerro La Silla** (easy, family-friendly): 360° views of the crater, cross at the top, great at sunrise.
  - **Cerro Cara Iguana** (moderate): ridge hike with views of the crater and town.
  - **Cerro Gaital** (very steep, rock scrambling, ropes): cloud forest, foggy summit, valley panoramas.
  - **Cerro La Gaita** (moderate-strenuous): jungle stairs, muddy/slippery sections, lookout platform.
  - **Cerro Turega** (strenuous, guide recommended): three peaks, river, waterfall, swimming.
  - **Pozo Azul** waterfall canyon: blue pools, cascades, swimming, cliff jumping.
  - **El Chorro Macho**: tall waterfall, canopy/zipline tour, rainforest trails.
  - **Golden Frog trail** (easy, flat): nature walk to see wildlife.
  - **Ruta de la Caldera**: route around the crater.
- **Attractions:**
  - **El Níspero Zoo & Botanical Garden:** 90+ species (monkeys, tapirs, toucans, macaws, big cats, crocodiles), orchids, a plant nursery, and the golden frog conservation center.
  - **Hot springs (Pozos Termales):** thermal pools and mud masks. A good rainy-day activity.
  - **Artisan & farmers market:** crafts, plants, orchids, fruits and vegetables.
  - Serpentarium, butterfly garden, orchid garden (orchid collections, including Panama's national flower, the Holy Spirit/dove orchid), petting farm.
  - **La Piedra Pintada:** petroglyph rock carvings.
  - Square trees, ziplining, rappelling, downhill mountain biking, bike rentals, birdwatching, village walking tours.
  - Day trips: nearby beaches, Laguna San Carlos, Cerro Picacho.
- **Language tasks:** greeting tourists, asking where they're from and where they're going; giving directions; recommending hikes by fitness level ("If you want something easy, try La Silla"); explaining difficulty, time, and what to bring (water, rain jacket, good shoes, sunscreen); telling the legend of La India Dormida; describing nature, wildlife, and history; guided tour language; explaining hours and entrance fees; selling crafts and souvenirs (prices, bargaining, materials, "handmade"); restaurant recommendations; safety tips (rain, mud, slippery trails, going with a guide).
- **Grammar:** questions (Wh- questions), directions and prepositions of place, descriptive adjectives, "you can / you should," present simple for facts.

## Unit 4: University English Preparation
- Focus on **academic English** for entering and succeeding at university.
- **Content:** academic vocabulary, reading comprehension strategies, summarizing, note-taking, essay structure (thesis, paragraphs, conclusion), formal emails to professors, presentations, class discussion phrases, test-taking vocabulary, application/interview language.
- **Grammar:** complex sentences, connectors (however, therefore, although), passive voice, formal vs. informal register, citing and paraphrasing.

## Unit 5: Vehicle Paperwork & Insurance
- **Vocabulary:** registration, license plate, driver's license, title/ownership, annual inspection, insurance policy, coverage, premium, deductible, claim, accident report, damage, liability, renewal, fine/ticket, receipt, "paz y salvo" (proof of no debts).
- **Language tasks:** helping an English speaker understand Panamanian vehicle procedures; filling out forms; explaining what documents are needed; describing an accident; making and following up on insurance claims; renewing documents; explaining fees and deadlines.
- **Grammar:** "need / must / have to," past tense for describing events, formal requests ("Could you please…").

## Unit 6: Hotel & Hospitality
- **Language tasks:** greeting and welcoming guests; check-in / check-out; taking and confirming reservations (phone, email, walk-in); explaining room types, amenities, prices, and policies; answering questions about the area; calling taxis and arranging transport; recommending tours and restaurants; handling complaints and problems (no hot water, noise, wrong room); room service and housekeeping requests; saying goodbye and asking for reviews.
- **Vocabulary:** single/double room, suite, availability, deposit, rate, Wi-Fi, breakfast included, late check-out, key card, amenities, front desk, luggage, towels, reservation number.
- **Jubilado discount:** explaining if and how it applies to rooms or services, asking for the retiree ID/carné, and showing it on the bill ("Do you have your jubilado card? You qualify for a discount on…").
- **Grammar:** polite forms ("Would you like…?", "May I…?"), future for plans, apologizing and offering solutions.

## Unit 7: Psychology (Family & Medical Psychology)
- **Topics:** the brain and nervous system, mental health conditions, trauma, abuse, neglect, family breakdown, divorce, grief, addiction, child development, anxiety, depression, therapy approaches.
- **Vocabulary:** diagnosis, symptoms, assessment, session, counselor, therapist, coping skills, support system, trauma-informed care, attachment, boundaries, domestic violence, mandated reporting, confidentiality.
- **Language tasks:** conducting intake interviews; asking sensitive questions respectfully; expressing empathy; explaining treatment plans; writing brief case notes; talking with families and children; referring to other services.
- **Grammar:** open-ended questions, present perfect ("How long have you felt this way?"), reported speech, softening language.
- **Note:** keep tone professional and sensitive; focus on professional communication, not graphic content.

## Unit 8: Finance & Accounting
- **Vocabulary:** income, expenses, budget, invoice, receipt, balance sheet, profit, loss, taxes, accounts payable/receivable, payroll, deposit, withdrawal, transfer, interest, loan, audit, fiscal year, currency (US dollar/Balboa).
- **Language tasks:** explaining a bill or invoice; talking with bank clients; describing financial reports; discussing payment terms and deadlines; answering tax and payment questions; writing professional finance emails.
- **Grammar:** numbers, percentages, and dates; comparatives (increased, decreased); passive voice ("The payment was received").

## Unit 9: Administration & Office Paperwork
- **Vocabulary:** form, application, signature, copy, ID/passport, appointment, deadline, file, stamp, requirements, approval, office hours, pending, submit.
- **Language tasks:** helping people fill out forms; explaining requirements and steps; scheduling appointments; answering phone calls and emails; giving updates on status ("Your application is still pending"); taking messages.
- **Grammar:** polite requests, instructions, present continuous for status, spelling names and numbers aloud.

## Unit 10: Daily Life & Social English
- **Language tasks:** greeting people (formal/informal); introducing yourself and others; starting and keeping a conversation going; small talk topics (weather, family, work, El Valle, food, hobbies, travel); asking follow-up questions; making friends and invitations; accepting/declining politely; ending a conversation; texting and social media language; shopping, the bus, the market, the pharmacy, asking for help.
- **Content:** cultural differences between Panamanian and North American/European social norms (personal space, directness, time).
- **Grammar:** present simple, question formation, "Would you like to…?", "How about…?", common expressions and idioms.

## Unit 11: Teaching & Learning Other Languages
- **Vocabulary:** grammar, pronunciation, accent, vocabulary, fluent, native speaker, conjugate, translate, practice, mistake, lesson, level.
- **Language tasks:** explaining Spanish to English speakers (and vice versa); giving instructions in class; correcting mistakes kindly; explaining meaning and usage; language exchange conversations; talking about learning strategies.
- **Grammar:** explaining rules in English ("We use ___ when ___"), comparisons between languages, imperatives for classroom instructions.

## Unit 12: Grocery Store
- **Vocabulary:** aisle, shelf, produce, dairy, meat/deli counter, bakery, frozen foods, canned goods, cleaning supplies, brand, price tag, sale/discount, in stock / out of stock, expiration date, cart, basket, bag.
- **Food terms:** fruits and vegetables (including local ones: plantain, yuca, ñame, papaya, pineapple, culantro), meats and cuts, weights (pound, kilo, half a pound), packaging (can, box, bottle, dozen, bunch).
- **Language tasks:** helping customers find products ("It's in aisle 4, next to the rice"); explaining what a product is or how it's used; deli/meat counter orders ("How much would you like?"); explaining sales and substitutes; handling requests for items that are out of stock; bagging and carry-out.
- **Punto de Oro (El Rey loyalty card):** asking if the customer has one, explaining what it is and how to sign up, and explaining points and benefits ("Do you have a Punto de Oro card? You can earn points on your purchases.").
- **Jubilado discount:** customers may ask for it. Students must politely explain that the grocery store does not offer it ("I'm sorry, the jubilado discount doesn't apply here, but…").
- **Grammar:** prepositions of place, countable vs. uncountable nouns (some, any, how much, how many), quantities and weights.

## Unit 13: Nail Salon & Hair Salon
- **Hair vocabulary:** haircut, trim, layers, bangs, blow-dry, straighten, curl, highlights, color/dye, roots, shampoo, conditioner, treatment, part, length, shave, beard trim.
- **Nail vocabulary:** manicure, pedicure, gel, acrylic, polish, file, shape (square, round, almond), cuticles, nail art, removal, fill.
- **Language tasks:** greeting and booking appointments; asking what the client wants and confirming details ("How much would you like me to cut?"); showing options and colors; checking comfort during the service ("Is the water too hot?"); explaining prices, time needed, and aftercare; handling unhappy clients; small talk during the appointment; taking payment and tips.
- **Grammar:** "Would you like…?", comparatives (shorter, lighter, darker), measurements (an inch, a little bit), yes/no confirmation questions.

## Unit 14: El Valle Market (Crafts, Plants & Produce)
- **Vocabulary:** handmade, handcrafted, woven, carved, painted, soapstone, wood, basket, hat, mola, pottery, jewelry, orchids and potted plants, seedlings, fresh vegetables and fruits, organic, local, bunch, pound, bag.
- **Language tasks:** greeting customers and inviting them to look; describing products (what it's made of, who made it, where it's from, how long it takes to make); explaining prices and making deals ("I can give you two for $15"); plant care tips for buyers; explaining whether plants can travel or be taken on a plane; describing vegetables and how to cook them; making change; wrapping items for travel.
- **Grammar:** passive voice ("It is made by hand"), numbers and prices, "can / can't," descriptive adjectives and order of adjectives.

## Unit 15: Waiter / Waitress
- **Vocabulary:** menu, specials, appetizer, main course, side dish, dessert, drinks, check/bill, tip, table for two, reservation, order, rare/medium/well-done, grilled, fried, spicy, vegetarian, gluten-free, allergy, refill, to-go box.
- **Local dishes:** sancocho, patacones, arroz con pollo, fresh fish, ceviche, empanadas, tamales, chicheme — how to describe them in English.
- **Language tasks:** greeting and seating guests; explaining the menu and describing dishes; taking orders and asking follow-up questions ("How would you like it cooked?"); handling allergies and special requests; checking on the table; fixing problems and complaints; bringing the check, splitting the bill, taking payment; saying goodbye.
- **Jubilado discount:** asking if anyone at the table qualifies, requesting the retiree ID, explaining how it appears on the check, and handling split bills where only some people qualify.
- **Grammar:** "Would you like…?", "Can I get you…?", describing food with adjectives, polite apologies and offers.

## Unit 16: Cashier
- **Vocabulary:** cash, card, credit, debit, change, receipt, total, subtotal, tax (ITBMS), discount, coupon, refund, exchange, return, bag, scan, void, cash register, "insert/tap your card," PIN.
- **Language tasks:** greeting customers; announcing totals clearly; asking about payment method; counting change aloud; handling returns, exchanges, and refunds; price checks and errors; explaining store policies; handling a declined card politely; saying thank you and goodbye.
- **Jubilado discount:** asking for the retiree ID, applying it, and explaining where it does or doesn't apply. At the grocery store, explain politely that it isn't offered.
- **Punto de Oro (El Rey):** asking "Do you have your Punto de Oro card?", explaining sign-up and points, and entering or looking up the customer's number.
- **Grammar:** numbers and money (dollars and cents), polite requests, short standard phrases, present simple for policies ("We don't accept returns without a receipt").
